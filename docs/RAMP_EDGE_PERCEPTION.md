# Ramp-edge perception integration

The current autonomous seed-2027 normal course **completed all 82 checkpoints**. Its [run](../artifacts/courses/seed-2027/run.json) and [12-check audit](../artifacts/courses/seed-2027/validation.json) passed with no reset, audit gaps or line-guard blocks. Maximum observed speed was 2.2 m/s, minimum sampled obstacle clearance 0.703277464 m and conservative swept clearance 0.536494889 m. These are ideal simulation measurements, not hardware fidelity or competition speed-compliance evidence.

The nominal 6 m painted corridor tapers to stripe centers at Y=±1.44 m on the 3 m wide ramp. Stripe outer edges reach ±1.5 m; shared vertices follow the rise, deck and descent continuously. No parallel outer wide paint remains. [Course-ramp geometry](COURSE_RAMP.md) is supplied to rendering and support simulation, not perception.

Depth classification uses organized sensor returns, local normals and observed connectivity. The near fitted plane seeds support membership; it is not extrapolated globally across every visible surface. Steep walls, isolated box tops and missing-depth gaps remain excluded from connected ground. This is a conservative geometric model, not semantic proof that every connected shallow surface is traversable.

`/perception/depth/surfaces` publishes odom `PointCloud2` observations with four FLOAT32 fields: `x`, `y`, `z`, `ground` (0 or 1), using a 16-byte point stride. Its stamp matches the depth fit status. RGB projection joins an available surface/status pair preceding its exposure by at most 0.35 simulation seconds, with receipt freshness at most 0.5 monotonic seconds. RGB CameraInfo and optical-to-odom TF match the RGB acquisition stamp exactly.

`project_observed` reprojects observed world points into the RGB camera, z-buffers nearest optical depth and selects support within eight pixels. All observations participate in occlusion; lane output requires the selected visible point to be ground-eligible. Output follows the queried RGB ray at the selected observed depth, with no infinite-plane fallback. Dark-hazard pixels use all observed points rather than ground eligibility. Sparse support and independently timed exposures still approximate local geometry; unsupported pixels produce no point.

Validation passed 58 perception tests, ten generator tests, 70 Unity ramp checks and the build. The captured failure fixture that produced 335 legacy plane-based obstacle points produced zero in the final surface replay. The [earlier baseline](TERRAIN_PERCEPTION.md) remains dated historical evidence rather than being erased or relabeled as a pass.

The [45-second read-only observer](../artifacts/checks/ramp-edge-live.json) started on the return leg at approximately (−9.56,14.67) and ended near the finish (−0.2937,−0.00698); it did **not** observe the ramp traversal itself. It received 16,430 left-region and 18,300 right-region repeated lane samples, 208 valid depth fits, zero invalid fits and zero depth obstacle points, with no missing streams or decode errors. Consequently it observed zero surveyed-ground obstacle marks during that interval. Both edge regions were represented, but retained historical points and unidentified return sources prevent treating these counts as fresh ramp-edge detections. XY error was at most 0.2596 m (p95 0.207 m); Z error was at most 0.2485 m (p95 0.164 m). These errors do not establish calibrated or exact edge placement; sparse pixel support and accumulated observations remain limitations. Survey geometry exists only in this verifier, never autonomy. The completed 82-checkpoint run is the primary autonomous integration evidence; this observer is a separate, limited interval.

This loop does not establish arbitrary-terrain performance, actual tire contact, traction, suspension calibration, stereo uncertainty or real negative-obstacle detection. Further coverage should include occlusion, missing depth, surface discontinuities, misleading shallow object tops and varied lighting.
